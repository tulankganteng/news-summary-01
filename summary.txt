World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026

1. US-mediated talks between Russia and Ukraine entered a second day in Abu Dhabi, the first three-way talks since February; President Volodymyr Zelensky called day one "constructive" and said a prisoner swap was raised (BBC, Reuters). Russian strikes hit substations in 10 Ukrainian regions, killing two; Russia said it shot down 92 drones over Leningrad (Reuters).

2. The US House blocked a bid to impeach Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth, rejecting Republican Thomas Massie's resolution; all Republicans bar Massie voted against it (BBC).

3. The US Justice Department charged three Iran-backed men over alleged plots across Europe, including one near RAF Fairford, a UK base used by US forces (BBC). 18 plots are alleged.

4. A 41-year-old senior US Army soldier was arrested in Britain on suspicion of spying for Russia (BBC).

5. World leaders began two days of speeches at the COP31 climate summit in Antalya, opened by Turkey's Erdogan and UN chief Guterres (Reuters). Brazil's Lula, the EU's von der Leyen and China's Han Zheng spoke on Wednesday; the US stayed away for a second year.

6. Nvidia shares fell 5.5% more on Tuesday, to $168.80, as its board named ex-US attorney Joon H. Kim to investigate chief executive Jensen Huang's affair with a woman US officials believe was a Chinese spy (Reuters, BBC). A shareholder suit followed on Monday; Huang says he did not know, and China calls the claims groundless.

7. El Salvador's military destroyed the HQ of Haiti's 400 Mawozo gang, its president said (BBC).

8. A week after the US midterms, the Associated Press had Democrats on 254 House and at least 54 Senate seats, with 16 races uncalled (AP).

9. London's Privy Council struck down Trinidad and Tobago's law criminalising gay sex, ending Jason Jones's 11-year legal battle (Reuters, BBC).

10. Nine Kennedys sued to block President Donald Trump's planned 250-foot arch in Washington, saying it would shadow John F. Kennedy's grave (BBC).
